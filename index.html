UX CASE STUDY · INDEPENDENT CONCEPT

Opportunity should
feel accessible.

How might a career platform support confidence and clarity for young job seekers across Nepal—from the first search to a meaningful interview?

The design opportunity

Job seekers need direction

The concept explores discoverability, understandable role requirements and a single place to track progress. This is a hypothesis for future research, not a claim about measured issues in the existing Merojob product.

Recruiters need context

The paired employer view organizes candidates into concise evidence-oriented cards that support shortlisting and consistent decision making.

UX approach

01

Discover

Audit the current job and hiring journeys; interview recent graduates and recruiters.

02

Define

Prioritize search confidence, application transparency and bilingual inclusivity.

03

Prototype

Design responsive flows, a reusable system and realistic fictional content.

04

Validate

Test job discovery, application tracking and comprehension across languages.

05

Refine

Use observed errors and task completion evidence to revise hierarchy and wording.

06

Document

Present the design decisions, limitations and outcomes with honesty.

What would we test?

Can an entry-level candidate find a relevant role and confidently understand the next step in under two minutes?

Proposed research: 5–7 moderated candidate sessions, recruiter interviews, English/Nepali language review, keyboard accessibility checks, and comparison of search-task completion and comprehension. No research results are claimed as completed.
