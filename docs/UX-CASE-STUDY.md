# Merojob — UX Case Study

**An independent Nepal-first employment platform concept by Sabnam Pariyar.**

## Design question

How might we help a young Nepali job seeker discover a fitting opportunity, understand its requirements and move confidently through the application process?

Merojob is a real employment platform in Nepal. This exercise is an unofficial speculative interface concept. It does not claim measured faults in the current product.

## User journeys

1. **Discover:** Search job listings by keyword, city and sector; compare salary, skills and working arrangement.
2. **Save and apply:** Bookmark promising opportunities and submit a fictional demonstration application.
3. **Track:** See a consistent application timeline from Applied through Shortlisted, Interview and Offer.
4. **Build profile:** Update candidate details and follow the completeness indicator.
5. **Recruiter perspective:** Review fictional candidates and practice shortlisting decisions.

## Local design considerations

- **Nepali and English:** Both languages are represented in the interface using Noto Sans Devanagari and Latin typography.
- **Place-based discovery:** Sample filters cover Kathmandu, Lalitpur, Pokhara, Chitwan and remote work.
- **Local salary context:** Sample jobs show monthly amounts in Nepali rupees (रु.).
- **Reading clarity:** Status information uses written labels as well as color.
- **Cultural restraint:** Nepal's geography and everyday urban life inform the visual moodboard; decorative clichés do not drive interaction design.

## Design rationale

- Clear search controls encourage exploration.
- Job cards answer the first questions: role, city, work style, skills and salary.
- A separate applications view reduces uncertainty after applying.
- Candidate profile completion uses progressive guidance.
- Employer cards foreground skills and evidence-oriented shortlisting.

## Future research

A real next phase would include qualitative interviews with first-time job seekers and recruiters, moderated search/application tasks, bilingual content testing and accessibility review. Potential measures: task completion, comprehension of application status and error recovery.

No participant interviews, production conversion metrics or improved outcomes are claimed as completed.

## Scope

All jobs, businesses, candidates, salaries and application decisions are fictional. No account is created, no job is applied for and no employer is contacted. Photography comes from external Unsplash reference images.
